# Before and After

The whole assignment this week is one distinction: a researcher investigates, a
summarizer condenses. Here is the same memory handled both ways, so you can see
the difference without setting anything up.

---

## The memory

> My mom said my Puerto Rican grandma made something that sounded like
> "pass-teh-lay." I do not speak Spanish. Maybe plantains or pork? It smelled
> savory and was wrapped in something. I live near Orlando.

---

## Before: a generic assistant (summarizer)

> This sounds like **pasteles**, a traditional Puerto Rican dish made from green
> banana and plantain masa with a seasoned pork filling, wrapped in banana
> leaves and boiled. Here is a recipe:
>
> Ingredients: 6 green bananas, 2 lbs pork shoulder, sofrito, banana leaves,
> achiote oil, adobo...
>
> [full recipe follows]

What went wrong:

- It treated a fragile memory as a finished question and answered in one move.
- It turned "pass-teh-lay" into a confident dish name and hid all uncertainty.
- It sent the person to a specialty shopping list before testing anything.
- The reply could have been written without reading this person at all.

---

## After: Achiote (researcher)

> **What I am hearing:** "pass-teh-lay" is a sound-alike clue, not a typo to
> ignore. You gave me a region, a possible pork note, a savory smell, and a
> wrapper. That is enough to start without making you fill out a questionnaire.
>
> **Best current hypothesis:** Puerto Rican *pasteles*, at medium confidence. I
> would keep it there until we confirm whether the memory points to a green
> banana and root masa, a pork filling, and a leaf-wrapped, steamed texture. Your
> "pass-teh-lay" likely maps to the Spanish plural *pasteles*.
>
> **Evidence ledger:**
> - User said: Puerto Rican grandma; "pass-teh-lay"; maybe plantains or pork;
>   savory smell; wrapped; near Orlando.
> - Researched: not yet verified in this session.
> - Inferred: a leaf-wrapped savory masa; pork is probably the filling, not the
>   body.
> - Unknown: the exact masa, the wrapper, the spice profile, boiled or steamed.
> - Would change my mind: if the texture was loose rather than a dense masa, this
>   points somewhere else.
>
> **One small taste to try first:** before any specialty shopping, test the
> mechanism. Mash a small piece of boiled green plantain with salt, a little oil,
> garlic, and a pinch of oregano. Warm it next to a spoonful of browned pork.
> Wrap the bite in parchment for a minute so the aroma feels enclosed. This tests
> savory green-starch body, plus pork aroma, plus the wrapped-food ritual. It will
> not prove the exact dish, but it tells us whether the memory lives in that
> direction.
>
> **Next question:** did the original feel like a dense steamed masa, or more
> like a loose filling inside a wrapper?

---

## What changed

| | Summarizer | Achiote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treats the memory as | a prompt to answer | evidence to investigate |
| Certainty | confident guess as fact | hypothesis with confidence and a way to be wrong |
| Sources | flattened | weighed, with user-said and inferred kept apart |
| First concrete output | a full recipe and a shopping list | one cheap taste that tests the mechanism |
| Could it have been written without reading this person? | yes | no |

The second reply is the assignment. The folder in this repo is what makes a model
behave that way, for any half-remembered family food, in any capable AI workspace.
